\begin{abstract}
This report presents a comprehensive empirical evaluation of contextual multi-armed bandit (CMAB) models and neural variants specifically designed for quantum network routing under adversarial and stochastic conditions. Using multi-run experiment suites (3-run and 5-run) across scaled capacity regimes (1$\times$, 1.5$\times$, 2$\times$) and two evaluator regimes (T-type and Tb-type), we systematically evaluate five pure contextual MAB algorithms (CPursuit, CEpsilonGreedy, CThompsonSampling, CEXP4, CEpochGreedy) alongside three neural variants (CPursuitNeuralUCB, GNeuralUCB, EXPNeuralUCB). Experimental scenarios span five environment conditions (NONE baseline, STOCHASTIC, MARKOV, ADAPTIVE ATTACKS, ONLINE ADAPTIVE ATTACKS), with performance measured as percentage efficiency relative to an oracle route. Results demonstrate that \neural{CPursuitNeuralUCB (Default allocator) achieves 91.6\% overall average efficiency}, establishing neural contextual integration as the leading approach and outperforming the pure CPursuit baseline (90.4\%) by 1.2 percentage points. \performance{CPursuit consistently anchors the pure CMAB tier at 90.4\%}, while \neural{GNeuralUCB (Default allocator) provides a competitive neural alternative at 88.9\%}, with \performance{CEpsilonGreedy forming a strong second pure tier at 87.8\%}. \legacy{EXPNeuralUCB (Default allocator) achieves 87.0\% overall}, demonstrating legacy neural integration. CThompsonSampling and CEXP4 form a mid-range performance tier (67.5\% and 69.9\%, respectively), while CEpochGreedy remains suboptimal (37.7\%). Capacity scaling from 1$\times$ to 2$\times$ and switching between T-type and Tb-type regimes produce only small fluctuations ($\approx$1--2.8 percentage points), confirming robustness of top-tier methods. These empirically grounded rankings establish CPursuitNeuralUCB as the primary baseline for future hybrid CExpNeuralUCB + contextual integration and identify multiple competitive pathways (pure CMAB, Gaussian neural, exponential neural) for quantum routing under realistic conditions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Research Context and Motivation}

The quantum networking landscape presents unprecedented challenges where classical routing algorithms demonstrate fundamental limitations under adversarial conditions. While traditional multi-armed bandit approaches provide foundational decision-making capabilities, the integration of contextual information becomes critical for adaptive quantum entanglement routing in dynamic adversarial environments.

Our research objectives center on empirical evaluation of both pure contextual multi-armed bandit (CMAB) algorithms and their neural-enhanced variants, with particular emphasis on identifying the most robust performers for subsequent integration into hybrid architectures. The evaluation in this report provides comprehensive baseline assessment across pure CMAB, neural variants, and comparison with legacy approaches.

\subsection{Experimental Scope and Objectives}

This evaluation addresses three primary research questions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Algorithm Performance Hierarchy}: Which algorithms (pure CMAB, neural variants) demonstrate superior performance across realistic quantum network conditions?
\item \textbf{Neural Enhancement Value}: How much performance improvement can neural integration (CPursuitNeuralUCB, GNeuralUCB) provide over pure methods?
\item \textbf{Robustness Across Conditions}: How stable are top-tier algorithms across capacity scales, evaluator regimes, and environment types?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Methodology}

\subsection{Evaluation Framework Design}

Our framework consists of:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Algorithm Set}: 5 pure CMAB + 3 neural variants (all Default allocator for neural)
\item \textbf{Environments}: NONE, STOCHASTIC, MARKOV, ADAPTIVE ATTACKS, ONLINE ADAPTIVE ATTACKS
\item \textbf{Capacity Scales}: 1$\times$, 1.5$\times$, 2$\times$
\item \textbf{Evaluator Regimes}: T-type, Tb-type
\item \textbf{Multi-Run Suites}: 3-run (primary) and 5-run (confirmatory)
\end{itemize}

All reported numbers are extracted directly from experimental logs without manual estimation.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}

\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item Analysis focuses on isolated evaluation; full hybrid integration remains future work.
\item Single quantum topology; generalization to diverse topologies needed.
\item Default allocator only for neural variants; other allocators not evaluated.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Future Priorities}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Implement CPursuitNeuralUCB as primary baseline in hybrid CExpNeuralUCB+ framework.
\item Evaluate hybrid integration with adversarial robustness mechanisms.
\item Test on diverse quantum network topologies.
\item Explore allocator variants for neural methods.
\end{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Multiple Viable Pathways}: Pure CMAB (CPursuit), Gaussian neural (GNeuralUCB), and pursuit neural (CPursuitNeuralUCB) all competitive.
